The paper's headline · not our result

The claim that makes this paper exciting

Genetically lower non-HDL cholesterol acting through HMGCR (the statin target), NPC1L1 (ezetimibe) and CETP carries roughly 70–80% lower odds of dementia — large effects with tight intervals, in the very targets already known to protect against heart disease. That is the paper's strongest result, and it comes from its one-sample arm: ~500,000 people with genotypes, lipids and outcomes all measured in the same individuals (Copenhagen and UK Biobank).

That data is application-gated and, for Copenhagen, legally unshareable — so the figure below is the paper's published numbers, not ours. It is the headline we set out to chase and could not run. Everything after it is the two-sample arm that public summary statistics do support.

Mind the units. These one-sample ORs are per 1 mmol/L lower non-HDL; our two-sample ORs are per 1 SD. The magnitudes are not comparable — read directions, not sizes.

Primer

What Mendelian randomization does

Observational data can only show that low cholesterol and low dementia risk travel together. Mendelian randomization (MR) reaches for cause by using genetic variants as a natural experiment.

Because alleles are dealt out randomly at conception — independent of lifestyle, wealth, or later illness — a variant that nudges a trait up or down acts like a lifelong, randomized “assignment” to more or less of that exposure. Such a variant, used as a genetic stand-in for the exposure, is the study's instrument (an instrumental variable). If variants that lower non-HDL through a drug-target gene also lower dementia risk, that is causal evidence for inhibiting that target.

The estimate is essentially a ratio — (variant→outcome) ÷ (variant→exposure) — and those two associations can be measured in the same people, or in two separate studies. That single choice defines what public data can and cannot reproduce.

Result · the two-sample arm

Our reproduction against the paper

The fair comparison is our 2SMR against the paper's 2SMR — both per 1 SD lower non-HDL. IHD (ischemic heart disease) is the positive control: a well-established effect of anti-atherogenic targets that must appear before the dementia estimates are worth reading. Points left of the dashed line (OR < 1) mean lower risk.

For IHD the two series sit almost on top of each other — a near-exact reproduction of the control. For all-cause dementia the directions agree everywhere, but our intervals are wider and pull toward the null: our outcome is FinnGen alone (~19k dementia cases) where the paper's 2SMR also pooled UK Biobank and Copenhagen. NPC1L1 and PCSK9 were non-significant in the paper's 2SMR too. The largest gap is CETP — the same protective direction, but attenuated from the paper's 0.54 to 0.84 and no longer significant; since our CETP→IHD matches the paper closely, that attenuation points at the FinnGen dementia outcome rather than the instrument.

Robustness · phase 2

Does the estimate survive being stressed?

Target-specific exposures

Re-scaling each target on the lipid it primarily acts on (LDL, or logTG for ANGPTL4/LPL) leaves every direction unchanged.

Five estimators agree

For the multi-SNP genes (PCSK9, CETP), IVW, weighted median, weighted & simple mode, and MR-Egger all coincide within rounding.

Assumptions hold

MR-Egger intercepts ≈ 0 (little pleiotropy); Cochran's Q is clean except a mild PCSK9→IHD signal.

De-novo instruments

Re-deriving instruments by the paper's method (cis → GWAS-sig → clump r²≤0.05 → F>10) recovers every named SNP as a genuine cis signal; direction is unchanged.

Instrument strength was never a concern — per-SNP F ranged 62–492, all far above the conventional floor of 10.

Extension · not the paper's data

Adding cases from bigger AD studies

Extension, not reproduction. Bellenguez 2022 and Kunkle 2019 were not used by the original authors. This asks a different, stronger question — does the signal hold in larger, independent Alzheimer's GWAS? — and is reported separately.

Bigger AD samples sharpened the arm exactly as expected. The null targets became confident nulls (PCSK9 meta 1.02, 0.93–1.13), and ANGPTL4 crossed into significance in a harmful direction, consistent across all three GWAS.

Where the sources disagree, there are concrete reasons — not noise between equals. The sharpest case is HMGCR: protective/null in FinnGen (0.71) and Bellenguez (0.96) but significantly harmful in Kunkle (2.47). Two structural causes:

Single-instrument fragility

HMGCR, NPC1L1, ANGPTL4, LPL each rest on one variant (a Wald ratio), so a cohort's estimate is that lone SNP's outcome association — with no averaging to damp noise, it can flip sign by chance. The multi-SNP genes stay stable across all three sources.

Different AD phenotypes

FinnGen is registry-diagnosed; Kunkle clinically adjudicated; Bellenguez adds by-proxy cases (UK Biobank family history) — a noisier, diluted phenotype. So systematic gaps reflect what “AD” means in each study, which is why we lean on the meta (clean Q) over any one source.

Precision improved — but ANGPTL4 stays single-instrument, so precision, not validity, is what grew. Treat it as a lead for a multi-instrument follow-up, not a finding.

Context · the arm we could not run

Why we did not reproduce the one-sample arm

One-sample MR needs every participant's genotype and lipids and dementia status in one dataset — which exists only in Copenhagen (Danish law forbids sharing) and UK Biobank (application required). Public summary statistics give per-variant β/SE only, which support two-sample MR but not one-sample.

That is why the headline figure at the top of this page is the paper's, not ours. With ~500k individuals and a per-mmol/L contrast it carries far more power than a single FinnGen outcome: our two-sample arm reproduces its direction, and matches the well-powered IHD control almost exactly, but cannot on its own deliver the same dementia significance.

Fine print

Documented deviations from the paper

  • Outcome = FinnGen only — no UK Biobank or Copenhagen, so wider CIs and attenuated dementia effects.
  • Per 1 SD lower exposure (GLGC betas are per-SD), not the one-sample arm's per 1 mmol/L.
  • logTG sensitivity reported per-SD, not per-halving of triglycerides.
  • Unspecified-dementia omitted — no clean standalone FinnGen R12 public phenocode.
  • The extension uses AD GWAS the authors did not use — reported separately, never merged into the reproduction.
